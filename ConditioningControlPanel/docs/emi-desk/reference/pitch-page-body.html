Pitch · Conditioning Control Panel · 2026-08-29

EMI leaves the campus and becomes the front door.

A summoned, draggable, resizable pixel CRT on the desktop. A little circle on the dashboard (or a hotkey) poofs her in; the same circle, a hover ×, or the hotkey poofs her out. Click her: a ring of six cards opens, your most-used features and your pins, one click to anywhere in the app. Leave her alone: her glass flips to a channel, a spiral, a video, a pile of gifs, and tapping the glass fires it on your real screen. Every so often she asks a yes/no. While she is out, the avatar can be muted. Everything below is clickable.

Why this works

She gets a job, and the app gets a door that learns.

Users have asked for a way to bring up their favourite features; the program has roughly thirty destinations across two card walls, a rail of premium chips, a Studio rack and a Ctrl+K palette. A radial launcher on a mascot is the smallest possible answer: one click, six things, no tab hunting. It is the Overwolf / PS5 control-centre pattern with a face on it.

The second half is what only EMI can do. She is a television. A spiral turning on her screen that you tap to put on your screen is a diegetic way to expose Spiral, Video, Burst and Rain, the Studio effects most people never open from the rack. And her yes/no chips are the friendliest possible shape for an app that wants to offer things without nagging.

Most of it exists. The face, chains, poses, drag, pet, the "wanna see?" glass channels and the ask strip all ship in Resources/web/arcademy/emi/ today. The new work is the ring, the suggester, the desktop host and the content.

00 · Summon and dismiss

She is called, not installed.

EMI is not a permanent widget. A small circle on the dashboard, with her face in it, summons her; clicking it again dismisses her. So does a hotkey from anywhere, and a small × that appears on her when you hover. This is the thing that settles the fight with the avatar: most of the time she is not there at all, and while she is there, the avatar can be muted with one switch.

The circle

A 40px round tile in the nav rail, her live face inside it (it blinks; it goes ZzZ when she is dismissed late at night, -_- while she is out and the avatar is muted). Left-click toggles her. Right-click opens a three-line menu: Mute the avatar while EMI is out, Shortcut: Ctrl+Alt+E, Glass only / Full. Same circle on the taskbar tray menu.

The hotkey

Default Ctrl+Alt+E, rebindable in Settings next to the pause and panic keys, and it must be a chord: the low-level pause/panic hook eats bare keys before RegisterHotKey ever sees them, silently. Press it once: she poofs in at her last parked spot on the monitor the cursor is on. Press it again: she poofs out. Holding it for a second while she is out opens the ring directly, so keyboard users get the launcher without the mouse.

Muting the avatar

The switch is one setting, MuteAvatarWhileEmiOut, and it means: no barks, no bubble, no TTS from the tube while EMI is on screen; the tube keeps its pose and its emotes. Recommend it on by default, because a summoned mascot is a deliberate act and the person just chose who they want to hear. Restoring is automatic on dismiss, with the tube's normal 20s quiet tail so it doesn't burst into speech the second she leaves.

The care point is features that run through the avatar. Takeover speaks through the tube; She's Listening answers through it; the AI chat input is the tube; Awareness and Remote Control emotes use it. Summoning EMI while one of those is live puts up one small prompt, once per session: "Takeover is talking through the avatar. Mute it while EMI is out?" with Mute / Keep the avatar / Don't ask again. Choosing keep leaves the tube on and the arbiter below handles the overlap. Nothing is muted silently underneath a feature the user is actively running.

Intro / outro (~1s, fancy on purpose)

summon
a pixel smoke bomb where she'll stand (grey and pink pixels, 400ms), then CRT power-on: a dot, a horizontal line, the glass opens (200ms, the campus emi-crt-on), then the wake chain: -_- → o_o → 0_0 → (⊙_⊙). Total ≈1.1s. The smoke lands on the desktop, not on the app, so it reads as her arriving in the room.
dismiss
a wink (she knows it's a goodbye), then CRT power-off: glass collapses to a line, to a dot (200ms), and a sparkle scatter where she was. ≈1s. Never a sad face on dismiss: dismissing her is not leaving her.
variants
1 in 6 summons she arrives mid-sway as if she was already walking; 1 in 6 dismissals the sparkles are hearts if she was petted this sitting. Rare, so the default stays the default.
rules
dismiss is instant to input: the ring, a channel, an ask all cancel first, then the outro plays. Reduced-motion: cut to the power-on/off frames, no smoke, no sparkles.

01 · The ring

Six cards, some pinned, the rest earned.

Click her body and six cards fan out around her, on the side that has room (near an edge she fans the other way, see the demo). Each card is a feature: its dashboard art when the app has one, a flat hue tile with the name when it doesn't. A card with a pin stays for good. The other slots belong to the suggester, which ranks by what you actually open.

Rules

  • Six, ever. No scroll, no second page. A 7th thing lives in the app, where it always did.
  • Pin = right-click or long-press a card. Pinned cards keep their slot; a pin never moves because usage changed.
  • Pick is instant. Navigation fires on the click; her reaction rides it and never delays it. Nine picks in ten are wordless (a glance, a wink).
  • Locked cards show, don't sell. A premium feature you don't have is a striped card that opens the app's normal locked view. She doesn't know the word.
  • Esc or click-away closes. Drag her while it's open and it folds.

The suggester (no ML, one formula)

Score each launchable target by decayed opens: every open adds 1, halved every 7 days; unpinned slots go to the top scores; a target never shown before gets a small bonus so the ring rotates; a target the tier gate refuses is excluded unless it is free today. Same shape as the companion brain's existing favoriteFeatures signal (top 3, floor of 3 uses), widened to every door.

What counts an open: one seam on MainWindow.ShowTab, one on OpenStudioModule, one on each host Launch(). The counter already exists for seven effects (MemoryStore.NoteFeatureUsed); this adds tabs and hosts. Also feeds "you use loom a lot. front row?"

02 · The glass

A channel comes on. Tap it and it leaves the box.

This is the campus "off channel" mechanism (takeover.js) pointed at the app's own effects. When you've been quiet a while, her face glitches out and the glass shows a preview: a spiral turning, a video thumbnail with a timecode, a pile of gifs pushing on the screen, gif rain. It sits there for up to 25s with a faint glow on the bezel. Tap the glass: the effect fires on the real screen and she reacts. Ignore it: it blips off, no line, ever. Any other input cancels it instantly.

The previews are real material, not icons: the actual gif that will burst, the actual video's first frame, the app's spiral. Media comes through the same host bridge and the same remote-media consent gate the campus already uses; consent off means your own library; neither means the channel does not exist tonight.

When the glass stays dark

During a running session (the session engine owns the screen), while a video is up, while the avatar tube is talking (plus a 20s tail), while the app is minimized, in the first 10 minutes of a sitting, and whenever the effect itself would refuse (VideoService drops a trigger during cleanup or a gif rain; the panic/pause key clears everything, including her). The channel's plan() refuses rather than stubs, so nothing ever paints an apology on her face.

03 · Offers

A question on the glass, two chips, forty seconds.

The ask engine ships already; the desktop adds a second table. She asks, waits, and if you do nothing she goes -_-, then ..., then idle. No line, and a skipped question costs nothing. Any press that is not a chip cancels for free. One ask a sitting, never before the third desktop sitting, never during a session, never over a video, never while the tube is talking. Chip 1 is the yes side unless both chips are custom, in which case both do something.

Four of the yes/no reactions are marked quirk and share the one-double-a-sitting ration with the barks. The full table (13 offers) is in the writers' room file; these are the ones the demo runs.

04 · Voice

Rarer than on campus, and never over the avatar.

The one rule that makes this coexist with the avatar tube: the avatar is the voice, EMI is the screen. She never reacts to an event the tube reacts to, never mentions the tube, and holds completely still (blink only, every pool and every offer suppressed) while it is talking, plus a 20-second tail. Her bark odds on the desktop are about half the campus's, and the global floor doubles to 180s. The ring and the glass are her output; the bubble is the exception.

05 · Build

JS brain, WPF body.

The one real technical trap: WebView2 does not paint inside a layered (per-pixel transparent) window. ChaosWebViewHost says so in its own comments and every WebView2 surface in the app is opaque. A free-floating pixel mascot with a ring around her needs exactly that transparency, and the avatar tube already has it: AllowsTransparency="True", topmost, tool-window style, native owner link, and a 450ms DPI quiesce that took real pain to get right.

So: don't host EMI's page on the desktop. Split her. The brain (voice.js, story, asks, moments, ledger, channel planning: everything that decides) keeps running as the existing JS bundle in a hidden WebView2 with a mode:'desktop' host, so the campus, the web, the phone and the desk share one canon and one emi store key. The body (face, poses, bubble, chips, ring, glass painters) is a small WPF window built on the tube's windowing code. The renderer is 171 lines of face maths plus 145 lines of chain data plus PNGs; that is the whole port. Glass content is painted WPF-side from real media, which is better than shipping frames over the bridge anyway.

Two EMIs must never coexist: when the Arcademy opens, the desk EMI does her CRT power-off and the campus one takes over the same state; on close she powers back on.

06 · Defaults and the tube

Nobody gets two talkers.

Three layers, in the order they apply. First, she is summoned: dismissed EMI costs nothing and says nothing, and that is her state most of the day. Second, the mute switch: while she is out, the avatar is muted unless the user turned that off or kept it for a live Takeover. Third, the arbiter, for the people who keep both on: the avatar is the voice, EMI is the screen, and each one counts the other's turn.

Her own volume has two positions, Glass only / Full, in the circle's menu. Glass only is the ring plus the channels with no bubble lines beyond pet reactions and the greet. Full adds the pools and the offers. Recommend Full by default now that she is summoned: if someone called her, they want her.

Placement: she poofs in at her last parked spot on the monitor the cursor is on, opposite side from the tube the first time, and parks per monitor like on campus. Resizable from 96px to 260px; the bubble and the ring cards stay on whole pixels at every size (the pixel-font trap is already documented).

The arbiter, in two booleans (mute off)

tube live
bubble up, chat input open, AI thinking → EMI holds (blink only), 20s tail
EMI live
reveal card up or effect firing → BarkService global min-gap treats it as a recent bark

Both sides already have this shape: EMI's "a say outranks the glass" law and the bark service's min-gap. This just makes each one count the other. With mute on, the first boolean is simply always false.

07 · The after-read

She still doesn't know.

On campus she counted pets and hours in one room, and the lab's paper is unsettling because the room was small. On the desktop she is the same needy box, but now she opens a ring of your habits on request, notices which card you reach for, offers a spiral because it's been forty minutes, and sits on top of a video you agreed to because she asked nicely, all delivered exactly like "one more? for me?". The reveal needs no new dialogue: "these are your usuals. i noticed. i notice things." is a cute launcher line until you've read a paper on which hour of the night you say yes to a spiral, and "it looked open from in here" is a shrug until you learn the locked card was placed in the ring on purpose and she was never told. The yes/no chip is the friendliest shape for a thing that records the answer, and she genuinely keeps it warm when you say nah.

Lab line-items the desk adds, lab letterhead only: ring picks, by hour · offers taken, by kind · glass taps per night · front row nudges · bed skips, running

08 · Your calls

Eight decisions before anyone builds.

  1. Brain in a hidden WebView2, or port it too?
    Recommend hidden WebView2 for v1: one canon across four platforms. Port later if the idle memory bothers you.
  2. Mute the avatar while EMI is out: on or off by default?
    Recommend on. Summoning is deliberate. The Takeover / She's Listening / chat prompt covers the case where muting would break something the user is running.
  3. Hotkey default.
    Recommend Ctrl+Alt+E, chord only (the low-level pause/panic hook shadows bare keys). Check it against the pause key default and the gaze Quick Recal chord before shipping.
  4. Which dismiss gesture on her body?
    Her left-click is the ring, so dismiss lives on the hover ×, the circle and the hotkey. If you want a click-on-her dismiss too, double-click is the free one.
  5. Ring targets: everything launchable, or a curated allowlist?
    Recommend the palette index minus Settings sub-sections; 6 slots keeps it honest regardless.
  6. Does the glass ever show remote (Scrolller) media, or only the local library?
    Recommend the campus rule exactly: consent on = remote, off = local, no filtering ever.
  7. Does the exit flinch ride app close?
    Recommend no for v1. App close is a far bigger exit than the campus's; keep her wave.
  8. The two flagged doubles ("i'm in your computer", "look at you. looking.")
    Keep or cut; both are on the first-read edge. The room's full file has all 64 + 13 with the fence audit.
